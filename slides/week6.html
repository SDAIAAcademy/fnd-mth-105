Block A · inclusion-exclusion

Add the sets, then subtract what you counted twice

|A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B|

|A| = 60, |B| = 50, |A ∩ B| = 20: |A ∪ B| = 90.

Three sets: |A ∪ B ∪ C| = |A| + |B| + |C| − |A ∩ B| − |A ∩ C| − |B ∩ C| + |A ∩ B ∩ C|

Why it works

An element in exactly two of three sets is added twice and subtracted once; one in all three is added three times, subtracted three times, added once. Every element ends with count 1.

Numbers from slide_numbers.py, week 6. The card is the proof: check each element's contribution. With 120, 90 and 60 in the sets, pairwise 30, 20 and 15, and 5 in all three, the union holds 210.

Checked with sets

A formula over counts can be checked against the sets themselves

ie = len(A) + len(B) - len(A & B)
assert ie == len(A | B)
  • The formula needs only counts: useful when the sets are held by different teams
  • The union needs the elements themselves
  • On data you hold, compute both: they must agree

The general rule alternates signs over every sub-collection of the sets; PS6 asks for a function that does it for any number of sets. The complement trick of week 5 is the same idea: count what you do not want and subtract.

Block B · counting people

Quasi-identifiers single people out in combination

Direct identifier

Identifies a person alone: a name, a member number, a code assigned to one person. "Removing names" removes these columns.

Quasi-identifiers

Shared by many people alone, but rare in combination: birth date, sex, postal district. Often known to neighbours and colleagues.

The question to count. After the direct identifiers are gone, how many people are alone in their combination of quasi-identifiers?

The lab's register has no names at all: its direct identifier is the volunteer code. Removing that column is the "removing names" of the course description. The point holds either way.

Block C · the law

Data that can identify a person indirectly is personal data

Personal Data Protection Law, Article 1

Personal data includes data that "may directly or indirectly make it possible to identify an individual" (SDAIA's English text).

SDAIA's anonymisation guideline

Anonymised data "shall no longer be considered personal data"; the guideline asks that it be irreversible, with an assessment of re-identification. It states that it is not a binding legal document.

So. An extract in which birth date, sex and district single people out still makes identification indirectly possible: removing the code column does not anonymise it.

Sources: the Personal Data Protection Law, Article 1, and the Personal Data Destruction, Anonymization and Pseudonymisation Guideline, version 1.0, both on sdaia.gov.sa; quoted from the English texts, which the course read. The guideline also says that replacing an identifier with a code (pseudonymisation) leaves the data personal. Quote, do not paraphrase into legal advice.

The guideline lists generalisation (age bands) and aggregation (birth year instead of birth date) among its example techniques; the lab tries five releases of the centre's register and measures each one.

Linkage

Releases combine: the risk is the union, not the safer of the two

  • Two tables, each with few people alone, are released: A and B are the people alone in each
  • Anyone in A ∪ B can be found: |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B| people
  • A list published elsewhere with the same columns links back row by row

Count before you publish

For each row of a list to be posted, count how many people in your register share its quasi-identifiers. One means that row names a person.

Inclusion-exclusion from block A, applied to people. The lab links a sign-in sheet the centre posted last year back to its register.

Checkpoint

Is it anonymised?

A gym removes member numbers from its list and keeps full birth date, sex and home district. Most members are alone in their combination. Under the Law's definition, the list is:

anonymised, since no identifier remains
still personal data, since it makes identification indirectly possible
pseudonymised, so outside the Law
personal data only if more than half are unique
Why. The definition covers data that may indirectly make identification possible, which unique combinations do.

Answer B. C is wrong twice: this is not pseudonymisation, and the guideline says pseudonymised data remains personal data.

Week 6 · wrap-up

Three things to keep

Subtract the overlap

Inclusion-exclusion counts a union from its parts, and code can check it.

Count who is alone

Removing the identifier column leaves quasi-identifiers; count the classes of size 1.

Releases add up

Two safe-looking tables, or a posted list, can name people together.

Ask: which list in their own organisation would they now count before publishing?
