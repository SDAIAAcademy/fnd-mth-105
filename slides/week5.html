Where is the hypothesis used?

To prove 1 + 3 + 9 + … + 3ⁿ⁻¹ = (3ⁿ − 1) / 2 by induction, the step for k + 1 must:

check the formula for n = k + 1 by computing both sides for k = 5
assume the formula for k + 1 and simplify
replace the first k terms by (3ᵏ − 1) / 2, add 3ᵏ, and simplify to (3ᵏ⁺¹ − 1) / 2
test the formula for n up to 1,000
Why. (3ᵏ − 1) / 2 + 3ᵏ = (3 · 3ᵏ − 1) / 2: the hypothesis supplies the first k terms.

Answer C. Ask a participant to say aloud where the hypothesis enters: the rubric's "an inductive step that uses it".

Block B · collisions

All-different assignments are an arrangement count

  • n people, each given one of N codes: Nⁿ assignments
  • All codes different: N (N − 1) … (N − n + 1) = P(N, n)
  • Chance all different = P(N, n) / Nⁿ, a count divided by a count

Checked by listing

6 codes, 3 people: listing all 216 assignments finds 120 with no repeat, and 6 · 5 · 4 = 120. The chance is 120 / 216 = 5/9.

Numbers from slide_numbers.py. The chance assumes every assignment is equally likely, which a good hash makes nearly true. The lab compares formula and brute force for every N up to 7.

Pairs

The average number of colliding pairs is C(n, 2) / N

  • Each of the C(n, 2) pairs shares a code in N · Nⁿ⁻² of the Nⁿ assignments
  • So, averaged over all assignments, a pair collides 1/N of the time
  • 30 people, 100 codes: C(30, 2) / 100 = 4.35 colliding pairs on average

A count, not a simulation

The average is a total over every assignment divided by their number. Brute force on small cases checks it exactly.

This is a counting argument, swapping the order of a double sum: count (assignment, colliding pair) pairs by pair first. Simulation with error bars is a different method, taught in the probability course.

Block C · pigeonhole

More items than boxes means some box holds two, and ⌈n / N⌉ is guaranteed

  • 1,000 people and 366 possible birthdays: some day holds at least ⌈1000 / 366⌉ = 3 people
  • Proof by contradiction: if every box held at most ⌈n / N⌉ − 1, the boxes would hold fewer than n items
  • Below n = N, nothing is guaranteed, though collisions may be likely

In hashing

Any map from long codes to short codes squeezes many inputs into each output: collisions are certain, only their rate is in question.

Week 2's unsatisfiable schedule was a pigeonhole case: two shifts each needing one of the first-aiders who can work them, and only one such person. Ask who remembers.

What is guaranteed?

A depot sorts 830 parcels into 100 bays by the last two digits of the parcel number. What does the pigeonhole principle guarantee?

Some bay receives at least 9 parcels
Every bay receives 8 or 9 parcels
Some bay receives at least 8 parcels, and no more is guaranteed
Nothing, since 830 is less than 100 · 100
Why. ⌈830 / 100⌉ = 9: if every bay held at most 8, the bays would hold at most 800 parcels.

Answer A. C starts with a true statement but then denies the stronger guarantee of 9: rounding down is the error.

Three things to keep

Multiply, add, divide

Product for steps, sum for disjoint cases, divide by the orderings you do not want.

Pairs drive collisions

C(n, 2) / N pairs on average; trouble starts near the square root of N.
