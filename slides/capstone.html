Test first, then prove. A passing test is evidence for the cases it ran; only the proof covers the rest.

The full criteria are in the README; the bars shorten them. An invariant proof with initialisation, maintenance and termination counts as the induction proof. The portfolio is 30% of the course and the defence 10%, scored separately, so nothing counts twice. Full marks on validity: a reader could check every line without asking the author.

Format

10 minutes for each defence

1 · The claim

The assessor picks one of your three proofs. State the claim and the method you chose. About one minute.

2 · Step by step

Walk through the proof and say why each step holds, then what your tests showed. About six minutes.

3 · A changed hypothesis

The assessor changes one assumption. Say whether the proof survives and which step breaks. About three minutes.

Peer dry run. Pairs defend one proof to each other and change one hypothesis for each other. The partner writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

Take the changed hypotheses from the trainer pack, prepared per claim. Typical changes: a weaker base case, a domain that now includes 0 or negative numbers, a strict inequality made non-strict. A good answer names the step that uses the assumption.
